Where I would invest your first 14 hours with Claude

I would start with one decision a business owner can act on. By the end of the engagement you should know which work to automate, what it costs to operate, and what evidence would justify expanding to a second business.

My opening recommendation

Start with the dealership close if the GM is waiting on a margin explanation and the controller can verify it. An earlier acquisition deadline and a checkable downside case could put underwriting first.

Choose the first business decision

In the 30-minute fit call, show me a decision that arrived late or needed rework. In separate discovery, we identify an approved source, a trusted reference and the person who can act. Among ready projects, you choose the most consequential decision before its deadline.

  1. Dealership margin

    Choose it when a better brief could change the next operating meeting. If the report already arrives on time and the real delay is a pricing decision, investigate that delay first.

  2. Acquisition underwriting

    Choose it when committee is approaching and a dated rent roll and expense statement can support a downside case. Ask which occupancy assumption could actually change whether the deal advances.

  3. Cross-business reporting

    Defer consolidation until entity owners agree on metric definitions, periods and access. Reuse the reporting method while keeping each business’s data and approval authority separate.

Discovery deliverable. You receive the first choice, the alternative we deferred, what would reverse the order, a baseline, and the minimum benefit worth funding. I prepare the workshop around your approved example.

Use the workshop to test the investment

  1. Day one · Prove the use case 7 hours

    We compare your existing report with a Claude workflow on the same case. You build the entity context and reusable Claude Code skill. Code reconciles the numbers. Claude drafts the explanation. We count preparation, review and corrections through acceptance.

  2. Day two · Prove you can operate it 7 hours

    We map the relevant HubSpot, QuickBooks and Microsoft 365 sources, test a scoped MCP read and challenge the draft with a reviewer agent. You catch a wrong entity, recover a failed run and repeat from a fresh session. Your controller or investment lead owns release.

  3. Optional day three · Resolve one uncertainty 7 hours

    Choose a private-host and connector rehearsal if deployment is the constraint, or a housing sensitivity case if transferability is the question. Finish at 14 hours if another day would not change the investment decision.

Decide whether to expand, revise or stop

Test the economics. In a fictional close, manual preparation and review take 80 + 10 = 90 minutes. With Claude they take 30 + 65 = 95 minutes. Faster drafting adds five minutes to total work. I would revise before proposing expansion on time savings.

At the next real decision, run alongside the current process. Compare the result with your agreed value target, quality checks and operating cost. Expand to one more entity when those hold and its owner verifies access. Retest a fixable shortfall. Stop if the benefit cannot justify the burden.

You keep the skill, reviewer definition, connector map, recovery runbook and sponsor decision log. The sponsor owns funding, the business owner signs off the figures, and the platform owner accepts operating costs and recovery.

Example pilot · A dealership month-end brief

These results come from three recorded calls to the lab’s read-only MCP server. The server read fictional records. Open the calls below or reproduce the session in your own checkout.

Ask for North Store’s September figures

get_reporting_packet("north-store", "2026-09") returned $250,000 in income and $207,500 in cost of goods sold. Gross profit was $42,500. At the policy floor, it would have been $45,000.

The figures trace to QBO-PL-09, POL-MARGIN-04 and CRM-SNAPSHOT-09. Inspect the source records.

Ask for another business or reporting period

The same server rejected South Store for September and North Store for August. Both requests returned an error before releasing a financial packet. Ask the executive to predict the outcome, then inspect the returned values. Open all three recorded calls.

Review a plausible explanation for the miss

An example draft says discounting caused the decline. The packet contains a policy comparison, one month of financial totals and open deals. It contains no pricing bridge or prior-month results. The reviewer should return the sentence for revision and ask the controller for the breakdown.
